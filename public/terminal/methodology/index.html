Methodology

"Tested. Graded. Exposed." means one thing here: we put the label claim next to the lab's own number, using the published rules below. It is not an accusation about any company's intent.

Where the data comes from

Every hemp record is a COA a brand published on its own site. Licensed-dispensary records are publicly posted batch COAs (see below). We read each one by hand and record only numbers printed on it. Anything not on the COA stays blank (n/a), never estimated. Every record links to the original COA.

Display rounding

Lab numbers are shown rounded to 1 decimal (exact integers without a decimal; non-zero values under 0.05 shown as "<0.1"). Scores and grades are computed from the unrounded values. The linked COA always has the exact figures.

Total THC

Total THC = Δ9-THC + 0.877 × THCA when the COA reports both; otherwise the lab's own reported total, with the basis noted.

Label accuracy

A COA is scored only when the label claim and a tested amount use the same unit (for example mg Δ9 per gummy). accuracy % = tested ÷ claim × 100; deviation % = accuracy − 100. On label = within ±10%; under label = more than 10% below; over label = more than 10% above.

Honesty score & grade

score = max(0, 100 − mean |deviation %|) across a brand's scored COAs. Grades: A+ ≥ 97 · A ≥ 93 · A− ≥ 90 · B+ ≥ 87 · B ≥ 83 · B− ≥ 80 · C+ ≥ 77 · C ≥ 73 · C− ≥ 70 · D+ ≥ 67 · D ≥ 63 · D− ≥ 60 · F < 60. Brands with no scorable COA are not graded.

Leaderboards

  • Most Honest: brands with at least 3 scored COAs, ranked by score.
  • Most inflated vs. label: brands with at least 3 scored COAs and at least one COA testing more than 10% below its label claim, ranked by the share of scored COAs that tested under label, then by average deviation. It describes test results only.
  • Brands with 1–2 scored COAs are graded but listed as "small sample", not ranked.

Stickers

  • Honest Label: score ≥ 90 with at least 3 scored COAs.
  • Avg under label: mean deviation ≤ −10%.
  • Avg over label: mean deviation ≥ +10%.
  • Dec 11 Watch: at least one COA over 0.4 mg THC per container.

Dec 11 watch

The new federal hemp definition takes effect Dec 11, 2026, with a limit of 0.4 mg total THC per container. Synthetic cannabinoids lose hemp status on Nov 12, 2026. We flag only when the COA lets us compute it, using the first rule that applies:

  1. The COA reports THC mg per package (plus Δ8 mg if reported).
  2. THC mg per unit × units per package.
  3. A single unit is already over 0.4 mg.
  4. Δ8 mg alone is over 0.4 mg.

Otherwise the flag stays blank. It is informational, not a legal determination.

Licensed dispensary · CO/MI

Products from state-licensed marijuana brands in Colorado and Michigan carry a Licensed dispensary · CO/MI tag. Sources, in order: state regulator data (used only as aggregate state benchmarks, never as a brand score), batch COAs that are publicly posted, and a label claim matched to the same batch. A COA with no matching label claim is shown but not scored. These brands use the same formulas and the same 3-COA minimum, but they get their own Dispensary tab and never go on the hemp boards. The federal Dec 11 per-container hemp limit does not apply to state-licensed marijuana, so these products are never flagged for it. Few licensed brands post COAs publicly, so coverage is thin.

Limits

COAs are batch-specific and brand-supplied. Small samples swing scores. Transcription errors are possible: send corrections.
