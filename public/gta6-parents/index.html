When it comes out, and where

GTA 6 launches November 19, 2026. At launch it is on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S.

What Mature 17+ means

M stands for Mature 17+. The ESRB uses it when a game is meant for ages 17 and up. An M game can include intense violence, blood and gore, sexual content, and strong language.

The rating is a warning label. It does not stop the game from starting, and it does not mean every 17-year-old should play it. It means younger kids are outside the age the rating was built for. You still make the call for your house.

What each content warning means

The ESRB lists these descriptors for GTA 6. Each line is the plain-language meaning of that label.

Blood and Gore
The game shows blood and graphic injuries.
Intense Violence
Violence, including gunfights and other attacks, is a regular part of playing, not a rare cutscene.
Mature Humor
The jokes are written for adults and can be crude or mean.
Nudity
Some scenes include nudity.
Strong Language
Characters use strong profanity, and they use it often.
Strong Sexual Content
Sexual situations are explicit, not a quick joke or a fade to black.
Use of Drugs and Alcohol
Alcohol and drug use are part of the world and the story.
In-Game Purchases
The game can offer optional things to buy with real money, so a saved card can be charged if spending is left open.

What's in the game

GTA 6 is an open-world action game set in Leonida, a fictional version of Florida, including the city of Vice City. The leads are Lucia and her partner Jason. You drive, walk, and take on story missions in a large map.

That is the high level. The rating looks the way it does because crime, violence, sex, language, drugs, and alcohol are part of that fiction, not a switch buried in a menu.

Why the console settings matter

A rating tells you what is in the game. It does not lock the game. On a shared PlayStation 5 or Xbox Series X|S, a child who can open the console can start an M-rated game unless that child's account is restricted.

Both consoles can limit which age ratings a child account can play. They can also limit online play and spending. PlayStation and Xbox do this in different places. The step-by-step version is in the paid guide. It is not written out here.

Settings are a backstop for the decision you already made. If the answer is no, a restriction can block a purchase or a launch on a child's account. If the answer is yes for an older teen, spending and online chat are still worth closing off. A passcode can be shared or guessed, so a setting works best next to a clear house rule.
